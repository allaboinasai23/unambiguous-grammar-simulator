Compare the Ambiguous Grammar (G₁) and Unambiguous Grammar (G₂) simultaneously on the exact same mathematical expression.

Benchmark Expressions:

Ambiguous Grammar (G₁)

G₁ = ({E}, {+, *, (, ), id}, R₁, E)
2 Parse Trees

Production Rules

E → E + E | E * E | ( E ) | id

Generated Parse Trees & Interpretations

Ambiguity Analysis: In G₁, both + and * are introduced at the same nonterminal E without hierarchy. Consequently, the parser can reduce either addition first or multiplication first, yielding two valid derivations with differing arithmetic values.

Unambiguous Grammar (G₂)

G₂ = ({E, T, F}, {+, *, (, ), id}, R₂, E)
1 Unique Parse Tree

Stratified Production Rules

E → E + T | T   (Addition)
T → T * F | F   (Multiplication)
F → ( E ) | id   (Atomic)

Enforced Canonical Grouping

Unambiguity Guarantee: Multiplication * is locked within non-terminal T, which is a child of E. This guarantees multiplication is reduced first. Left recursion (E → E + T) ensures identical operators associate strictly left-to-right.
Formal Synthesis & Mathematical Conclusion

For the expression 'id + id * id', the Ambiguous Grammar yields 2 distinct parse trees whereas the Unambiguous Grammar yields exactly 1 parse tree. This conclusively illustrates the disambiguation property: introducing non-terminals (T, F) stratifies operators into strict precedence tiers, eliminating multiple syntactical interpretations.
