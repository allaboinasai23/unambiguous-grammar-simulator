Unambiguous Grammar (G₂) Recommended

E → E + T | T  |  T → T * F | F  |  F → (E) | id

Enforces strict operator precedence (* over +) and left-associativity.

Ambiguous Grammar (G₁) Demonstration

E → E + E | E * E | (E) | id

Demonstrates ambiguity: permits multiple parse trees for expressions like id + id * id.

Active Grammar: Unambiguous G₂

Tuple: G₂ = ({E, T, F}, {+, *, (, ), id}, R₂, E)
E → E + T | T   (Tier 1: Addition)
T → T * F | F   (Tier 2: Multiplication)
F → ( E ) | id   (Tier 3: Atomic Operands)

Precedence Logic: Non-terminal T (Terms) generates multiplication operations deeper in the parse tree than E (Expressions). Because reductions occur bottom-up, multiplication is always evaluated prior to addition.

Active Grammar: Ambiguous G₁

Tuple: G₁ = ({E}, {+, *, (, ), id}, R₁, E)
E → E + E | E * E | ( E ) | id

Why G₁ is Ambiguous: The grammar allows both E + E and E * E without precedence ranking or directional associativity. An expression such as id + id * id can be derived with addition at the root or multiplication at the root.
